If you are planning a commercial rooftop or a utility-scale solar farm, you will hear the term **EPCC** early in the process. It describes the full scope a contractor takes on, from first drawing to the day the system starts exporting power.

## Engineering

Site survey, energy yield simulation, structural checks and the electrical design. Decisions made here (module type, inverter sizing, string layout) shape how the plant performs for the next 25 years.

## Procurement

Sourcing modules, inverters, mounting structures, cables and protection equipment. Good procurement balances price against warranty terms, supplier track record and delivery dates.

## Construction

Mechanical and electrical installation on site, with safety management, quality checks and coordination with the building owner or landowner.

## Commissioning

Testing every string and inverter, verifying protection settings, and completing the documentation needed to connect to the grid and hand over to the owner.

## Why one contractor for all four?

When one party owns the whole chain, there is a single point of responsibility for performance, schedule and defects. Split contracts often lead to gaps where each party points at the other.
