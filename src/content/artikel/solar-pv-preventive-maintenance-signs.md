A solar PV system has no moving parts, so it is easy to assume it looks after itself. In practice, small issues build up quietly and show up as lost energy months later. Here are five signs it is time to call your O&M team.

## 1. Energy yield is dropping with no change in weather

Compare this month's kWh against the same month last year. A steady fall that weather does not explain usually points to soiling, shading from new growth, or failing strings.

## 2. Inverter alarms keep coming back

Repeated isolation faults, grid trips or communication errors are rarely random. Resetting the inverter clears the alarm but not the cause.

## 3. Uneven string currents

When one string produces noticeably less current than its neighbours under the same irradiance, check for damaged connectors, blown fuses or module defects. An **IV curve test** shows exactly where the loss sits.

## 4. Hot spots on thermal images

A drone or handheld thermal scan picks up cells running much hotter than the rest of the module. Hot spots cut output and, left alone, can damage the module permanently.

## 5. Visible damage after storms or roof works

Loose clamps, cracked glass and chewed cables are common after heavy weather or other contractors working on the roof. A quick visual inspection after these events saves bigger repairs later.

## What a preventive maintenance visit covers

- Visual and mechanical inspection of modules, mounting and cabling
- Inverter health check and firmware review
- String testing and IV curve analysis
- Thermal imaging
- Cleaning, where soiling losses justify it
